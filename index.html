Returns are percentages on the basis named under each book; they are not comparable across bases and must not be added up; max drawdown is measured on the same basis as the return, so a per-trade or per-closed-trade drawdown is not comparable with a whole-account one. Max drawdown counts from the start of each row's window. Ledger days = days with a ledger entry (trading days for stock and gold books, every calendar day for crypto). "Last 30 days" is shown only for running books with at least 30 days of history. A book that moved from live to paper is shown as two rows (the live row in its live section, the paper row under Paper), one per part, each measured from its own start; do not add them. Models are updated from time to time; each curve is the book as it ran. † = short sample (see below).

Method

How these are computed

  • Return and max drawdown: on the basis named under each book, from the start of the row's window. There is no annualised return (CAGR) on purpose: stretched to a year, a few good weeks look like a huge yearly number.
  • Sharpe: average daily return divided by the standard deviation of daily returns, times the square root of the number of ledger days per year (365 for markets that trade every day, 252 for weekday markets, read from the book's own calendar). No risk-free rate is subtracted. Sortino: the same, but dividing only by the downside (days below zero). Sharpe needs at least 30 daily returns; with 30 to 89 it is marked † (short sample: easily off by a lot either way). Sortino needs at least 90, because with few losing days it swings to very large numbers. A book that is often flat, or that only books results on the days trades close, has few moving days, which can make both ratios look better than they are.
  • Calmar (annualised return divided by max drawdown) appears only for books with at least 180 days.
  • Win rate: share of closed trades with a positive result. Profit factor: sum of winning trades divided by the sum of losing trades. Both need at least 10 closed trades; the count is shown under the win rate.
  • Best day and worst day: the largest daily gain and loss on the row's curve.
  • All figures come from the same ledger rows as the curves; record.json carries them (schema 3 since 2026-11-27, with the counts and ranges of the summary lines; older snapshots in the chain are schema 1 or 2 without some of these fields and stay valid).
